# Brundlefly at SemEval-2016 Task 12: Recurrent Neural Networks vs. Joint Inference for Clinical Temporal Information Extraction

## TL;DR
Two systems were submitted to SemEval-2016 Task 12: Clinical TempEval challenge using recurrent neural networks and joint inference for clinical temporal information extraction.

## Problem
Temporal information extraction is crucial in medical reasoning systems and longitudinal research, but unstructured text in electronic medical records makes it challenging. The task involves identifying text spans of time and event mentions, as well as predicting relations between events and document creation time.

## Method
Two approaches were used: a vanilla recurrent neural network (RNN) with word embeddings and a joint inference-based approach using structured prediction with DeepDive. For Phase 2, a combination of date canonicalization and distant supervision rules was used to predict relations on both events and time expressions.

## Results
The joint inference-based approach outperformed the RNN for temporal entity extraction, while the combination of date canonicalization and distant supervision rules improved classification for document creation time relations, but gains were limited due to small training data scale.
