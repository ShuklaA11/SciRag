# Many Languages, One Parser

## TL;DR
A multilingual model is trained for dependency parsing, enabling effective parsing across multiple languages.

## Problem
The paper addresses the problem of developing tools for processing many languages in NLP, which has been a long-standing goal. The mainstream approach to multilingual NLP involves designing language-specific models, but this approach is suboptimal due to theoretical and practical reasons.

## Method
The proposed model integrates several recent developments in dependency parsing, including universal POS tagsets, cross-lingual word clusters, selective sharing, and multilingual word embeddings. The parser takes as input sentences in multiple languages, optionally predicting part-of-speech tags and input language. It is trained on the union of available universal dependency annotations in different languages.

## Results
The parser compares favorably to strong baselines in three data scenarios: when the target language has a large treebank, a small treebank, or no treebank for training. The performance of the parser is not specified in terms of specific numbers or metrics.
