# What we write about when we write about causality: Features of causal statements across large-scale social discourse

## TL;DR
This study analyzes causal statements made on Twitter to understand how language and sentiment biases affect online communication of cause and effect.

## Problem
The paper addresses the problem of understanding how humans communicate causality online, which is important for evaluating the accuracy of information found online. Despite previous research on causal inference in science and philosophy, little is known about causal attributions made via social media or online social networks.

## Method
The authors extracted a large corpus of causal statements from Twitter using natural language processing tools and compared them to a control corpus. They used statistical language analysis and sentiment analysis to identify differences between causal and control statements.

## Results
Causal statements on Twitter tend to be more negative in sentiment than controls, and have significant lexical and grammatical differences. Causal statements focus on news, current events, medicine, health, or interpersonal relationships.
