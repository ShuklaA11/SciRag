# Revisiting Summarization Evaluation for Scientific Articles

## TL;DR
A study on the effectiveness of ROUGE, a widely used text summarization evaluation metric, for scientific articles.

## Problem
The authors address the problem of evaluating the quality of automatically generated summaries for scientific articles using ROUGE, which relies solely on lexical overlaps and may not be effective in cases of terminology nuances and paraphrasing.

## Method
The study compares ROUGE scores with semi-manual evaluation scores (Pyramid) on a TAC 2014 scientific summarization dataset, showing weak correlations between ROUGE and Pyramid scores. The authors then propose an alternative metric, SERA (Summarization Evaluation by Relevance Analysis), which is based on content relevance analysis.

## Results
The study reveals that ROUGE has weak correlations with manual evaluation scores for scientific summaries, while the proposed SERA metric achieves higher and more consistent correlations with semi-manual assessment scores.
